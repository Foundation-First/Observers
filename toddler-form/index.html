VQB5 Local CLASS Observation Report

We appreciate the opportunity to observe the interactions between you and the children in your care today. Positive, responsive interactions help children develop secure relationships, build communication skills, explore their environment, and support healthy learning and development. VQB5 uses the nationally recognized Classroom Assessment Scoring System (CLASS) to measure the quality of educator-child interactions that are linked to positive outcomes for children.

The CLASS tool organizes effective interactions into broad areas, called domains, which are further divided into dimensions. These dimensions describe specific educator practices that support children's social-emotional development, engagement, communication, and learning in developmentally appropriate ways.

Understanding Your CLASS Scores

Trained and certified CLASS observers document the quality of interactions demonstrated during the observation and assign scores on a scale of 1 to 7 for each dimension. These scores reflect the extent to which observed interactions support positive experiences and outcomes for children and can help identify strengths and opportunities for continued growth.

CLASS score ranges
RangeScoresDescription
Low1-2Low-range scores indicate that interactions associated with the dimensions were limited, inconsistent, or rarely observed. As a result, children had fewer opportunities to experience the kinds of responsive, engaging interactions that support their learning and development.
Mid3-5Mid-range scores indicate that interactions associated with the dimension were present some of the time or for some children. These interactions provided children with opportunities to support their development and learning through the experiences were somewhat available to all children throughout the observation.
High6-7High-range scores indicate that interactions associated with the dimension were consistently demonstrated in the classroom experience. Children regularly experienced meaningful, responsive, and engaging interactions that support positive social-emotional language and cognitive development. These experiences were seen over time and reflected the experiences of all or nearly all children in the classroom.

Composite scores for each dimension are created by averaging individual cycle scores across the number of cycles of observation completed. Composite scores for each domain are created by averaging dimension composite scores across the number of dimensions within the domain.

Using CLASS Scores to Guide Improvement

Positive educator-child interactions support children's social-emotional, language, and cognitive development. Research shows that children in classrooms with higher CLASS scores experience stronger learning and developmental outcomes. CLASS provides a framework for identifying and strengthening the interactions that matter most for children’s growth, regardless of curriculum or program model. CLASS measures the overall experience of children in the classroom and is not designed to evaluate the effectiveness of individual educators.

Emotional and Behavioral Support

Dimension and IndicatorsComments from Observations

Positive Climate Reflects the connection between the educator and children and the warmth, respect, and enjoyment communicated by verbal and nonverbal interactions.

  • Relationships
  • Positive Affect
  • Respect

Negative Climate Reflects the overall level of expressed negativity in the classroom. The frequency, quality, and intensity of educator and child negativity are the key to this scale.

  • Negative Affect
  • Punitive Control
  • Teacher Negativity
  • Child Negativity

*Negative Climate is scaled in the opposite direction of the other dimensions: the higher the score, the greater the expressed negativity.

Teacher Sensitivity Encompasses the educator’s responsiveness to and awareness of children’s individual needs and emotional functioning. The extent to which the educator is available as a secure base (being there to provide comfort, reassurance encouragement).

  • Awareness
  • Responsiveness
  • Child Comfort

Regard for Child Perspectives Captures the degree to which the educator’s interactions with children and classroom activities emphasize children’s interests, motivations, and points of view and encourage children’s responsibility and independence.

  • Child Focus
  • Flexibility
  • Support of Independence

Behavior Guidance Encompasses the educator’s ability to promote behavioral self-regulation in children by using proactive approaches, supporting positive behavior and guiding and minimizing problem behavior

  • Proactive
  • Supporting Positive Behavior
  • Problem Behavior

Engaged Support for Learning

Dimension and IndicatorsComments from Observations

Facilitation of Learning and Development Considers how well the educator facilitates activities to support children’s learning and developmental opportunities. How the educator connects and integrates learning into activities and tasks should be included in this rating.

  • Active Facilitation
  • Expansion of Cognition
  • Children’s Active Engagement

Quality of Feedback Assesses the degree to which the educator provides feedback (in response to what children say and/or do) that promotes learning and understanding and expands children’s participation.

  • Scaffolding
  • Providing Information
  • Encouragement and Affirmation

Language Modeling Captures the quality and amount of the educator’s use of language-stimulation and language-facilitation techniques to encourage children’s language development.

  • Supporting Language Use
  • Repetition and Extension
  • Self- and Parallel Talk
  • Advanced Language
